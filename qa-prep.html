SCALE

How would this scale to 10× the data or users?

LEAD WITH

Cost and latency are already linear and measured, so the first 6× is a config change, not a rewrite. The interesting engineering starts after that — and it isn't more parallelism, it's not asking the same question twice.

THE PROOF

  • Unit economics hold across five different vendors: $0.059–$0.067 per account, $0.011 per call. I've swept 56 accounts in testing for $3.46 total.
  • A 200-account book is about 1,000 calls — roughly 10 minutes and $11 a sweep.
  • Measured throughput is ~1.6 calls/sec against Exa's 10 QPS ceiling — I found the ceiling by hitting 429s. That's ~6× headroom before rate limits bind at all. Today I stagger calls 0.13s apart under a concurrency cap of 8, with backoff-retry.

THEN GO DEEPER — THE THREE LEVERS, IN ORDER

  • Diff mode is the real lever. After the first sweep you never re-read the web: each nightly run sets startPublishedDate to the last run, so lanes return only genuinely new pages and you pay extraction on those alone. Call it an estimate rather than a measurement — 3–5× lower steady-state cost — and note it removes noise too, because a signal you already triaged stops re-alerting.
  • Tiered cadence and a two-stage funnel. Top of book daily, long tail weekly. Retrieval is cheap and reading is not, so a search-only pass can gate which pages are worth paying the summary extraction on.
  • Users, not data. The scan path is stateless; the only shared state is an in-memory rate limiter and a file cache, which become Redis and object storage behind a work queue. Per-tenant keys already exist — the app takes bring-your-own-key today. The genuinely interesting multi-tenant optimisation: two customers watching the same account retrieve overlapping pages, so you cache the corpus per URL and re-judge per tenant against their own schema. Retrieval shared, judgment private.
IF THEY PUSH
“What breaks first?”

Precision, not throughput. Ten times the accounts means ten times the absolute false positives at constant precision, and a CSM forgives a miss long before they forgive noise. That's why scoring is deterministic and auditable rather than a model's opinion — lane weights, recency decay, and a hard requirement that the account is literally named in the page text. At 10× I'd add per-lane thumbs up/down and fit the weights to each customer's feedback instead of my judgment.

DON'TAnswer with “add more workers.” They're testing whether you know where the cost actually is.
DIFFERENTIATION

Why Exa over a keyword search API, or just scraping it themselves?

LEAD WITH

Because three of my five lanes have no keyword form at all. The best find in the whole demo — HubSpot's engineering post about a 20-billion-vector in-house retrieval platform — contains neither the word “Exa” nor the word “churn”. There is no alert anyone could have written that catches it.

THE PROOF — THREE THINGS KEYWORD CAN'T DO

  • Can't ask it. “An engineering post about building in-house what we sell them” is meaning-shaped. Keyword search needs a string; this signal has none.
  • Can't scope it. The competitor lane is one semantic query scoped with includeDomains to exactly the rival domains — and that rival set came from findSimilar with nothing but the vendor's homepage. By hand that's a crawler fleet across ten sites, JS rendering, freshness SLAs, and a permanent maintenance owner.
  • Can't read it. This is the half people forget. Keyword APIs return links and a human still reads them. Exa returns the quoted sentence, the date, the source and a JSON verdict classified against my schema in the same call — the schema is the prompt. That's what turns a research chore into something a machine runs nightly.

THEN GO DEEPER

  • Name where keyword wins — it makes everything else credible: “If I needed exact-string matching, an error code or a SKU or a legal entity, keyword is better and cheaper and I'd use it. This problem isn't that.”
  • Build-vs-buy in one line: the DIY version is a crawler team and an index; here it's $0.011 a call and freshness is Exa's problem, not the customer's.
  • The credibility flex. “I'd also tell a customer what I learned the hard way: includeDomains is a strong preference, not a hard filter — off-list results backfill when in-domain matches run thin, so I re-enforce scope client-side. You only find that by building on it, and it's the kind of thing I'd flag on day one rather than let them hit in production.”
IF THEY PUSH
“Couldn't an LLM with web search just do this?”

It could do one account, interactively. It can't do 200 nightly with per-lane cost control, deterministic scoring and a citation on every claim — and the moment you want that, you're building exactly this, on a retrieval API. The judgment layer isn't the hard part; the scoped, fresh, structured retrieval underneath it is.

DON'TTrash Google or keyword tools broadly. Name the boundary instead — it reads as judgment rather than pitch.
ROI

What's the ROI story for the economic buyer, not the end user?

LEAD WITH

For the CSM it's time. For their boss it's net revenue retention and forecast integrity — so I'd lead with insurance economics, not efficiency: the annual cost is about four basis points of the ARR it's watching.

THE PROOF — SAY WHICH HALF IS MEASURED

  • Measured: $0.06 per account per sweep; $11 for a 200-account book.
  • Assumed, and say so out loud: 200 accounts at $50k average ARR is $10M under watch. Nightly sweeps run ~$4.1k a year — 0.04% of what they protect. One saved account pays for twelve years.
  • The time math, stated honestly: a proper manual check is ~45 minutes per account per month; across 200 accounts that's 150 hours a month, about 0.9 of a full-time person.
FIX BEFORE YOU PRESENT

The README and the slides prompt say “~19 person-weeks a year.” That's wrong — 150 hours a month is ~19 person-days a month, or ~45 person-weeks a year (0.9 FTE). I've corrected the README; correct the deck too. The right number is bigger, so the story improves — but don't get caught by a panelist doing the arithmetic.

THEN GO DEEPER

  • Don't oversell headcount savings — say the quiet part instead: “I won't claim I'm saving you 0.9 of a person, because nobody is doing this work today. The value isn't cost taken out, it's coverage you don't currently have — the 175 accounts nobody is assigned to watch.”
  • Speak the buyer's metric. Surprise churn wrecks a renewal forecast; a signal 90 days early turns a surprise into a plannable save. Forecast confidence is a board-level number, and NRR is what investors index the multiple on.
  • The second product is free. The counter-signals are already collected — the “mentions vendor” flags — so the same sweep inverted becomes expansion detection. Same cost, second revenue story.
  • Preempt the risk question. Nothing here fires an action; it puts a dated, cited page in front of a human. A false positive costs a CSM one click. A false negative is exactly the status quo.

Then don't give them one. The next build is a nightly diff into Slack and a risk field written back to the CRM, so it shows up inside the renewal workflow they already run. Zero data entry, no adoption curve — the product is the alert, not the app.

ROADMAP

What would you build next if you had another sprint?

LEAD WITH

One thing, and it isn't a feature — it's the delivery mechanism. A nightly diff into Slack plus a risk field written back to the CRM. Today this is a tool you have to remember to open; that makes it a workflow that finds you.

THE FOUR, RANKED — AND THE ORDER IS THE ANSWER

  1. Nightly diff → Slack digest. Only what's new since the last sweep. It's the difference between a demo and a product, and it's the same change as the cost lever in the scaling answer — one build, two problems solved.
  2. CRM write-back. Risk tier and the top evidence link as a field on the account, so it lands in QBR prep and the renewal forecast without anyone opening my app. This is what makes the economic buyer's story real.
  3. A feedback loop. Thumbs up/down per evidence card, per lane, per customer — then fit the lane weights to their data instead of my judgment. Right now those weights are a defensible guess; after a sprint of feedback they'd be evidence.
  4. Websets for discovery. Replace my two-source customer cross-reference with one filtered, enriched list — and it unlocks the inverse query: find me more accounts that look like my healthiest ones.

WHAT I'D DELIBERATELY NOT BUILD

Not a scoring model. The moment a verdict stops being a transparent sum of weighted, dated evidence, a CSM can't defend it to their VP — and a risk score they can't explain is one they'll ignore. Auditability is the feature.

IF THEY PUSH
“What's missing today that worries you?”

Entity resolution. Subsidiaries, rebrands and acquisitions break name matching, and my name gate is deliberately strict — I'd rather miss than cry wolf. That's the first correctness work I'd fund after delivery.

DON'TRecite a feature list. Naming what you'd skip, and why, reads as product judgment; naming five equal priorities reads as none.
