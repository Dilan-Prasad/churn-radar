SCALE

How would this scale to 10× the data or users?

LEAD WITH

Name the trap first: there's no big-compute problem here — a 200-account nightly sweep is about a thousand HTTP calls, a cron job and a queue. What actually binds at 10× is quota, then noise — in that order — and one architectural inversion makes the whole question smaller.

THE NUMBERS UNDERNEATH

  • Unit economics measured across five vendors: $0.011 per call, ~$0.06 per account, 56 accounts swept for $3.46. One sweep runs at ~1.7 calls/sec; Exa's ceiling is 10 QPS per key — I know because I found it with 429s.
  • So the concrete 10× case: a 2,000-account enterprise book is 10,000 calls — about 17 minutes at the rate limit and ~$110 a sweep, ~$40k/year nightly against $100M of ARR watched. The cost is scale-invariant at ~4 bps, because cost and coverage both grow with the book.

THE TWO BINDS, IN THE ORDER THEY BIND

  • Quota — and it shards by design. Be honest that the ceiling is per KEY: one key sustains about six concurrent sweeps, so 10× tenants breaks a shared key immediately. The answer is already in the app: per-tenant bring-your-own-key means quota, billing, and usage attribution all shard per customer — ten tenants, ten ceilings, and each customer's Exa spend is their own clean COGS line. Above that it's a rate-tier conversation with Exa — "as the FDE, I'd be on both sides of that conversation."
  • Noise — the budget that actually runs out. Each account judges ~20 candidate pages; 200 accounts nightly is 4,000 page-judgments, and even a 2% false-positive rate is 80 wrong alerts a night. A CSM who gets a dozen wrong reds stops reading by Thursday. The controls: diff mode (below) kills repeat noise structurally, per-lane thumbs up/down fits weights to the customer's feedback instead of my judgment, and only strong-and-recent signals page a human — weak ones accumulate silently until they cross a threshold.

THE INVERSION — THE DEEPEST 30 SECONDS YOU CAN GIVE

  • Stop sweeping, start monitoring. Every run is date-windowed to "published since last run" via startPublishedDate, so the corpus you've already judged is never re-fetched. Steady-state cost stops tracking the SIZE of the book and starts tracking the rate of news about it — which is roughly constant and small per account.
  • Shared corpus, private judgment. Every vendor's book contains HubSpot and Notion, so at 10× tenants retrieval de-duplicates across customers — fetch HubSpot's engineering blog once — while judgment stays per-tenant: their lanes, their weights, their competitor set. Marginal cost per tenant falls as the platform grows.
  • What I'd delete, not multiply: the in-memory limiter, the file cache, the 2-scan cap are demo guards. Production is a work queue, a token bucket per tenant key, object storage. "The scaling exercise is mostly deleting my scaffolding."

OTHER WAYS IN — PICK BY WHO'S ASKING

  • The decomposition (technical panelist): "There are three different 10×s hiding in that question — accounts per customer, number of customers, sources per account — and they have different answers. Let me take them in order." Then quota / corpus-sharing / lane fan-out.
  • The invariant (commercial panelist): "Scale questions are usually cost questions in disguise, so here's the invariant: four basis points of the ARR it watches, at any book size."
  • The inversion-first (senior engineer): open with monitoring-not-sweeping and cost-tracks-news-not-book-size. Highest ceiling; needs confident follow-through.
  • The what-breaks-first ladder (default): "Nothing until ~6 concurrent sweeps on one key; then quota, which shards per-tenant; then noise, which is the real engineering; never compute — it's a thousand HTTP calls a night."
IF THEY PUSH
“What breaks first?”

Give the ladder in one breath: nothing until about six concurrent sweeps on one key → quota, which per-tenant keys shard → noise, which diff mode and the feedback loop control → and compute never makes the list. Then the trust point: a CSM forgives a miss long before they forgive noise, which is why every verdict stays a transparent sum of weighted, dated evidence rather than a model's opinion.

DON'TReach for Kafka and Kubernetes. Inventing heavy infrastructure for a thousand nightly HTTP calls is the junior tell this question is designed to catch — and don't say "6× headroom" without saying "on one key, for one tenant."
DIFFERENTIATION

Why Exa over a keyword search API, or just scraping it themselves?

LEAD WITH

Because three of my five lanes have no keyword form at all. The best find in the whole demo — HubSpot's engineering post about a 20-billion-vector in-house retrieval platform — contains neither the word “Exa” nor the word “churn”. There is no alert anyone could have written that catches it.

THE PROOF — THREE THINGS KEYWORD CAN'T DO

  • Can't ask it. “An engineering post about building in-house what we sell them” is meaning-shaped. Keyword search needs a string; this signal has none.
  • Can't scope it. The competitor lane is one semantic query scoped with includeDomains to exactly the rival domains — and that rival set came from findSimilar with nothing but the vendor's homepage. By hand that's a crawler fleet across ten sites, JS rendering, freshness SLAs, and a permanent maintenance owner.
  • Can't read it. This is the half people forget. Keyword APIs return links and a human still reads them. Exa returns the quoted sentence, the date, the source and a JSON verdict classified against my schema in the same call — the schema is the prompt. That's what turns a research chore into something a machine runs nightly.

THEN GO DEEPER

  • Name where keyword wins — it makes everything else credible: “If I needed exact-string matching, an error code or a SKU or a legal entity, keyword is better and cheaper and I'd use it. This problem isn't that.”
  • Build-vs-buy in one line: the DIY version is a crawler team and an index; here it's $0.011 a call and freshness is Exa's problem, not the customer's.
  • The credibility flex. “I'd also tell a customer what I learned the hard way: includeDomains is a strong preference, not a hard filter — off-list results backfill when in-domain matches run thin, so I re-enforce scope client-side. You only find that by building on it, and it's the kind of thing I'd flag on day one rather than let them hit in production.”
IF THEY PUSH
“Couldn't an LLM with web search just do this?”

It could do one account, interactively. It can't do 200 nightly with per-lane cost control, deterministic scoring and a citation on every claim — and the moment you want that, you're building exactly this, on a retrieval API. The judgment layer isn't the hard part; the scoped, fresh, structured retrieval underneath it is.

DON'TTrash Google or keyword tools broadly. Name the boundary instead — it reads as judgment rather than pitch.
ROI

What's the ROI story for the economic buyer, not the end user?

LEAD WITH

For the CSM it's time. For their boss it's net revenue retention and forecast integrity — so I'd lead with insurance economics, not efficiency: the annual cost is about four basis points of the ARR it's watching.

THE PROOF — SAY WHICH HALF IS MEASURED

  • Measured: $0.06 per account per sweep; $11 for a 200-account book.
  • Assumed, and say so out loud: 200 accounts at $50k average ARR is $10M under watch. Nightly sweeps run ~$4.1k a year — 0.04% of what they protect. One saved account pays for twelve years.
  • The time math, stated honestly: a proper manual check is ~45 minutes per account per month; across 200 accounts that's 150 hours a month, about 0.9 of a full-time person.

THEN GO DEEPER

  • Don't oversell headcount savings — say the quiet part instead: “I won't claim I'm saving you 0.9 of a person, because nobody is doing this work today. The value isn't cost taken out, it's coverage you don't currently have — the 175 accounts nobody is assigned to watch.”
  • Speak the buyer's metric. Surprise churn wrecks a renewal forecast; a signal 90 days early turns a surprise into a plannable save. Forecast confidence is a board-level number, and NRR is what investors index the multiple on.
  • The second product is free. The counter-signals are already collected — the “mentions vendor” flags — so the same sweep inverted becomes expansion detection. Same cost, second revenue story.
  • Preempt the risk question. Nothing here fires an action; it puts a dated, cited page in front of a human. A false positive costs a CSM one click. A false negative is exactly the status quo.

ROADMAP

What would you build next if you had another sprint?

LEAD WITH

One thing, and it isn't a feature — it's the delivery mechanism. A nightly diff into Slack plus a risk field written back to the CRM. Today this is a tool you have to remember to open; that makes it a workflow that finds you.

THE FOUR, RANKED — AND THE ORDER IS THE ANSWER

  1. Nightly diff → Slack digest. Only what's new since the last sweep. It's the difference between a demo and a product, and it's the same change as the cost lever in the scaling answer — one build, two problems solved.
  2. CRM write-back. Risk tier and the top evidence link as a field on the account, so it lands in QBR prep and the renewal forecast without anyone opening my app. This is what makes the economic buyer's story real.
  3. A feedback loop. Thumbs up/down per evidence card, per lane, per customer — then fit the lane weights to their data instead of my judgment. Right now those weights are a defensible guess; after a sprint of feedback they'd be evidence.
  4. Websets for discovery. Replace my two-source customer cross-reference with one filtered, enriched list — and it unlocks the inverse query: find me more accounts that look like my healthiest ones.

WHAT I'D DELIBERATELY NOT BUILD

Not a scoring model. The moment a verdict stops being a transparent sum of weighted, dated evidence, a CSM can't defend it to their VP — and a risk score they can't explain is one they'll ignore. Auditability is the feature.

IF THEY PUSH
“What's missing today that worries you?”

Entity resolution. Subsidiaries, rebrands and acquisitions break name matching, and my name gate is deliberately strict — I'd rather miss than cry wolf. That's the first correctness work I'd fund after delivery.

DON'TRecite a feature list. Naming what you'd skip, and why, reads as product judgment; naming five equal priorities reads as none.
THE STACK

What APIs were used where in the demo?

LEAD WITH

Don't answer this one from memory — answer it from the product. Every panel and every account row carries an API pill showing the verbatim request that produced it; click one, then give the map. Three endpoints, each worn several ways, and structured output on every single call.

THE MAP — IN PIPELINE ORDER

1 · GUARD/search ×1

Scoped with includeDomains to the typed domain: does this site exist in Exa's index at all? Without it, /answer cheerfully invents a plausible company for a nonsense domain — I found that by testing garbage input.

2 · VENDOR PROFILE/answer ×1

One call with an outputSchema: category, buyer, named competitors, and the capability phrase. Every downstream query is phrased from this object — it's why the same app asks search-infrastructure questions for exa.ai and deployment-platform questions for vercel.com.

3 · COMPETITIVE SET/findSimilar ×1

Competitor candidates from nothing but the homepage URL, each vetted by a schema summary ("is this a direct competitor?") before it joins the sweep — similar-looking is not the same as competing.

4 · THE ACCOUNT LIST/answer ×1–2 + /search ×2

Typed accounts resolve to canonical brand names via /answer (typo-tolerant — "open router" → OpenRouter), and open slots fill from a cross-reference: the vendor's own case-study pages (includeDomains on their site) against open-web coverage (excludeDomains), both extracted through schema summaries.

5 · THE SWEEP/search ×lanes×accounts

The core. Each call composes a semantic query from the profile, includeDomains scoped to rival domains on the competitor lane, category:news plus a date window on budget, and — the part to slow down on — highlights + a schema summary in the same request: find the page and read it in one call, returning the quote, the date, and a typed strong/weak/none verdict.

6 · KEY TEST/search ×1

The bring-your-own-key panel validates a pasted key with one minimal call before any sweep spends it.

RECONCILESthe demo run's summary strip reads 56 calls: 6 setup + 10 accounts × 5 lanes. The arithmetic checks out on screen.

THE SHAPE TO POINT OUT

  • One endpoint, many hats. /search appears five different ways — existence probe, case-study crawl, web cross-reference, signal lanes, key test — differentiated entirely by scoping and schemas, not by different products. That's the composability argument made concrete.
  • No raw text anywhere. Every call returns structured output — outputSchema on /answer, schema summaries on /search and /findSimilar. The schema doubles as the prompt; nothing downstream parses prose.
  • The drawer is the receipt. The under-the-hood console shows every call live with latency and per-call cost from the response's own costDollars — the $0.63 total is the sum of what's on screen, not an estimate.

It's on the roadmap because I know precisely where it slots. And the Research API, which is retired — I know because I probed the whole surface before building. Knowing what you didn't use, and why, is the difference between using an API and knowing one.
